The battle clips play in the order listed in `CONFIG.MUSIC.FILES.battle`

styles alternate on purpose, so two fights in a row never sound the same.

## The walking tune: an Oktoberfest medley

`overworld.mp3` is a four-minute medley of six songs you would actually hear in
a beer tent, opening with **Ein Prosit der Gemütlichkeit** — the toast every
Wiesn tent strikes up every fifteen minutes:

| # | Song | Melody | Score (CC0) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ein Prosit der Gemütlichkeit | Georg Kunoth (1863–1927) | <https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ein_Prosit_der_Gem%C3%BCtlichkeit.mid> |
| 2 | Bier her, Bier her | traditional | <https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bier_her.mid> |
| 3 | Muss i denn zum Städtele hinaus | traditional | <https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Muss_i_denn_zum_St%C3%A4dtele_hinaus.mid> |
| 4 | Im Krug zum grünen Kranze | traditional | <https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Im_Krug_zum_gr%C3%BCnen_Kranze.mid> |
| 5 | Auf de schwäbsche Eisebahne | traditional | <https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Auf_de_schw%C3%A4bsche_Eisebahne.mid> |
| 6 | Horch, was kommt von draußen rein | traditional | <https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Horch,_was_kommt_von_drau%C3%9Fen_rein.mid> |

Why this is free to use: the **melodies** are old enough to be public domain,
and the **four-voice arrangements and MIDI files** were made by Peter Gerloff,
who released them as CC0. There is no recording by anyone else involved — the
sound is generated by `source/make_medley.py`, which plays those scores on a
Game Boy-style band (pulse-wave trumpet, triangle-wave tuba). The scores are in
`source/` too, so the tune can be rebuilt or rearranged.

**You do not need any of that to run the game** — it just plays the mp3. To
rebuild after changing the song order or the sound:

    pip install mido numpy          # plus ffmpeg
    python3 assets/music/source/make_medley.py

(The earlier walking tune was `rpgchip03_town` from pack ¹ — a lovely RPG town
theme, but it sounded like a quiet village, not a Volksfest. It is still in git
history if anyone misses it.)

## How the battle clips were made

The originals are `.ogg` / `.wav`. Each was converted with

    ffmpeg -i <original> -af "volume=<N>dB" -codec:a libmp3lame -q:a 4 <name>.mp3

The only change is that volume nudge: the packs were mastered at very different
levels (Junkala's tracks are about 7 dB louder), so each clip was turned up or
down to sit at the same average loudness as `battle-1.mp3` (about -15.5 dB).
Without it the music would jump in volume from one fight to the next.
Gains used: battle-2 −7, battle-3 +0.8, battle-4 −7, battle-5 +1.5, battle-6 −6.6.
The walking medley is rendered to that same level.

These are original chiptunes in the Game Boy RPG style — deliberately NOT the
real Pokémon soundtrack, which is Nintendo's and can't live in a public repo.

## Want a different tune?

Drop the mp3 in this folder and add (or swap) its line in `CONFIG.MUSIC.FILES`
in `js/config.js`. The battle list can be any length. Add a row to the table
above so the next person knows where it came from and that it's free to use.
